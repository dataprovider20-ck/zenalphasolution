When an Amsterdam Company Needs Technical Leadership

Amsterdam attracts companies that hire across borders from their first year. The result is often a capable but scattered setup: freelancers in three countries, a design agency, a hosting partner and a backlog nobody truly owns. Decisions drift because no single person weighs them.

Our fractional CTO service gives that ownership a home. We keep one prioritised roadmap, run a short fortnightly review with your suppliers and write down every significant decision with its reasons.

  • One roadmap across in-house staff and suppliers.
  • Fortnightly supplier reviews with written actions.
  • Decision records that new hires can read.
  • Cloud and tooling cost checks.
  • A plan for moving work in-house when it makes sense.

Dutch Specifics Worth Raising Early

Introducing tools that can monitor employee performance may require the consent of a works council (ondernemingsraad) in larger Dutch companies, so we flag such tools before rollout. Software development can sometimes qualify for the WBSO research and development scheme; we keep tidy technical records and leave eligibility to your adviser. The Dutch data protection authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, oversees GDPR here.

How We Start With You

  4. A monthly report to the founders or board.

Cloud Costs and Vendor Sprawl in Amsterdam Scale-ups

Amsterdam has many companies that grew quickly on cloud services and now have a technology bill nobody fully understands. Growth hides waste. A test server left running, three tools that do the same job and an oversized database can add up before anyone notices.

A vCTO review starts with the invoices. We list every service, who uses it and what it costs per month, then mark each as essential, useful or unknown. The unknown ones are usually the quickest savings. We also look at how you are billed: some workloads suit reserved capacity, others do not.

Vendor choice has a second side. Some of your customers, especially in logistics, finance and the public sector, will ask where their data is stored and which companies can touch it. Keeping a clear register of suppliers, their locations and the data they handle makes those questions easier to answer and supports your obligations under European data protection law.

The outcome is a one-page cost map and a short list of changes, ordered by effort and saving. We do not recommend switching providers for its own sake. A migration has its own cost, and sometimes the right answer is to keep the current setup and tidy it up. You decide what to act on, and we help with the parts you want to hand over.

Amsterdam: frequently asked

What is the time difference with Amsterdam?

India is 4½ hours ahead of the Netherlands in winter and 3½ in summer, so most of our working day overlaps with yours.

Can you manage several suppliers at once?

Yes. Coordinating several freelancers and agencies is one of the most common reasons Dutch companies bring us in.

Do we need works council approval for new tools?

It depends on the tool and on your company. We point out tools that could monitor staff, and your HR or legal adviser confirms what is needed.

Do you speak Dutch?

We work in English, which most Amsterdam tech teams already use. Dutch-language interfaces can be built and tested with native reviewers.
