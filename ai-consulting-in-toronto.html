AI Work That Suits a Toronto Business

Toronto has deep research talent in machine learning, yet most local firms are not research labs. They run clinics, brokerages, logistics yards, agencies and retail chains, and their questions are modest: how do we answer customers faster, reconcile data between systems or stop re-typing forms?

Our consulting begins with those questions. We look at one process end to end, estimate the saving honestly and build a pilot only if the numbers justify it.

  • Customer question answering that links to your own help pages.
  • Reconciliation of orders, invoices and shipping data.
  • Transcription and summaries of internal calls, with consent handled properly.
  • Translation and tone checks for English and French messages.
  • Reports written from spreadsheet data in plain language.

Privacy and Language in the Canadian Context

Canada's federal privacy law, PIPEDA, governs how private-sector organisations handle personal information, and Quebec's Law 25 adds duties, including notice when a decision is made only by automated processing. Customers in Quebec also expect French.

We note where data is stored, who can read it and which tools see it, and we plan French-language testing when your audience needs it. Confirm legal obligations with your own counsel.

How a Toronto Engagement Starts

  1. A discovery call in your morning, our evening.
  2. A written pilot brief with the process, data and success measure.
  3. A prototype tested with a small group in your team.
  4. A short decision memo: scale it, adjust it or drop it.

Questions from Toronto clients

Do you only work with large companies?

No. Small and mid-sized firms often benefit most, because one automated process can free a noticeable share of a small team's week.

Can the pilot work in French as well as English?

Yes, for many tasks. We test quality in each language with your own examples before recommending it.

Where is our data processed?

We agree the hosting location with you before the pilot starts and document it, so you can answer customers and auditors. Some tools process data outside Canada, which is why we check early.

How do we measure success?

We agree one or two numbers beforehand, for example minutes saved per case or error rate, and report them at the end of the pilot.
