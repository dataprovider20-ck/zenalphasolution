Competing for Talent in Dublin

Dublin hosts the European offices of many of the world's largest technology firms, which makes hiring engineers here competitive. Smaller companies can wait months for a single hire, while the roadmap stalls.

A dedicated developer from our team works only on your product, in your repository and stand-ups. You interview each candidate and decide; we handle employment, equipment and continuity.

  • React and front-end specialists.
  • Python and back-end engineers.
  • Full-stack developers for complete features.
  • Mobile developers for iOS and Android.
  • QA engineers to strengthen testing.

Contracts and Access

Put three documents in place before day one: an IP clause making every line of code your company's property, a confidentiality agreement, and GDPR-compliant data processing terms where personal data is involved. Developers use accounts your company creates and can remove at any time.

Hiring Process

  1. Share the role, stack and working hours.
  2. Review shortlisted profiles and code samples.
  3. Interview and, if you wish, set a short paid task.
  4. Onboard with a checklist and a two-week review.

Working Across the Dublin and India Time Zones

India is five and a half hours ahead of Dublin in winter and four and a half in the summer. That sounds like a problem, but a dedicated developer's day overlaps with yours more than most teams expect.

A developer working 9:30 am to 6:30 pm India time is at the desk from 4:00 am to 1:00 pm Dublin winter time, so the overlap with a Dublin 9 to 5 day is about four hours in the morning, and a little more in summer. That window is the one for decisions: a short daily stand-up, code review discussion, and questions that need a quick answer.

Outside the overlap, work moves in writing. A good ticket describes the problem, the expected result and how to check it. A good pull request says what changed and why. These habits help local teams too, because they leave a record.

Plan a few rules on day one: which hours you are both reachable, how urgent issues are raised, and who approves releases. Some Dublin teams prefer the developer to work an hour later, which stretches the overlap to five hours, and that is something we can agree in advance. The result is often a rhythm where you assign work in your morning and review finished work the next day.

Questions from Dublin clients

Will the developer be online during the Irish day?

Yes for a large part of it. We put at least four shared hours into the contract, which is easy because Dublin mornings fall in our early afternoon.

Can I interview candidates myself?

Yes, you interview and choose every developer.

What if it does not work out?

Tell us early. We try to resolve it first and, if needed, bring in a replacement within the period set out in your contract.

Can we hire just one developer?

Yes. Many clients start with one and add more later.
