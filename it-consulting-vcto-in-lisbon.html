Virtual CTO for Lisbon Founders and Remote-First Teams

Part-time CTO guidance for companies built in or relocated to Lisbon, where teams are often remote, international and watching their budget closely.

Built for Distributed Companies

Lisbon has become a base for founders from across Europe and beyond, and its annual Web Summit keeps the city on the start-up map. Many companies here are remote-first from day one: a founder in Lisbon, developers in two or three countries and customers somewhere else entirely.

That model works well when someone owns the technical picture. We provide that ownership part-time and help you keep spending disciplined while the product finds its market.

  • A clear engineering process for a team spread across time zones.
  • Hiring plans that compare local, nearshore and offshore options honestly.
  • Monthly cost reviews of cloud services and tools.
  • Product and technical roadmap in one document.
  • Advice on when to stop building and start selling.

Portuguese and EU Basics

Portugal follows GDPR, supervised by the national data protection commission (CNPD), and the European Accessibility Act has applied to many consumer digital services since June 2025. If you sell online to consumers, we build accessibility and privacy into the plan rather than leaving them for later. Your legal adviser should confirm specific duties.

What the First Month Looks Like

  1. A call in the Lisbon morning, which is our afternoon.
  2. An inventory of everyone who touches the code and what they cost.
  3. A short written plan with three priorities.
  4. Weekly async updates and one live review each month.

Choosing a Stack for a Small Remote Team in Lisbon

Lisbon has attracted many small, remote-first companies, with founders and developers spread across several countries. For a team of three to eight people, the technology choices you make now will either speed you up or slow you down for years.

The first rule is to choose boring tools you can hire for. A mainstream language, a well-known framework and a managed database let a new developer be useful in days. Exotic choices might look attractive, but they make hiring harder and leave you dependent on one enthusiastic person.

The second is to automate the basics early: automated tests on every change, a repeatable deployment and a staging environment. A remote team cannot lean over a desk to ask whether something is safe to release, so the system has to tell them.

The third is to write decisions down. A short record of why you chose a tool, and what you would change your mind about, helps when someone new joins or when the team spreads across time zones. As a vCTO we help you pick the stack, set up the pipeline and write the first of those decision notes. We also help you decide when to buy a service instead of building it, so a small team spends its time on what makes the product different.
