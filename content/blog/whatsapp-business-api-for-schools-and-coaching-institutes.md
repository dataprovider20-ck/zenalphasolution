In many Indian schools and coaching institutes, WhatsApp communication still runs through a teacher's personal phone and a few large groups. It works until it doesn't: a teacher leaves and takes the contacts, parents miss a notice buried in a busy group, or a phone number ends up shared with hundreds of strangers.

The WhatsApp Business API offers a more organised approach. Messages come from one official number, several staff members can reply from a shared inbox, and updates go to each parent individually instead of to a crowded group.

## What schools and institutes typically send

| Message | Usual category | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Holiday and event notices | Utility | "School will remain closed on Monday for..." |
| Fee reminders | Utility | "Term 2 fee is due on 10 January. Pay online at..." |
| Test and result updates | Utility | "Unit test results are now available on the portal." |
| Admission campaigns | Marketing | "Admissions are open for the new session..." |
| Demo class invitations | Marketing | "Book a free demo class this Saturday." |

Keeping service notices and promotional messages separate matters, because WhatsApp treats them as different categories with different rules and costs.

## Why move away from groups

- **Privacy:** in a group, every parent can see every other parent's number. Individual messages from the API keep numbers private.
- **Ownership:** the number belongs to the institution, not to an employee.
- **Organisation:** a shared inbox lets the front office, accounts and teachers handle different questions.
- **Records:** you can see what was sent and whether it was delivered.

## Getting consent right

Collect a clear opt-in during admission or enrolment: a line on the form saying that the institution will send updates on WhatsApp to the number provided. Keep a record of it. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is also being phased in, and it places weight on clear notice and consent for personal data, so treat parent and student details carefully and ask your adviser how it applies to you.

For students under 18, communicate with parents or guardians rather than marketing directly to children.

## Useful automations

- An automatic reply that lists office hours and common links (fee portal, timetable, contact numbers).
- A simple menu: press 1 for fees, 2 for transport, 3 for admissions.
- Fee reminders sent a few days before the due date, then on the due date.
- Admission enquiries from your website or ads routed to the counselling team.

## Common mistakes

1. Sending promotional admission messages to every parent without consent.
2. Mixing fee reminders with offers, which changes the message category.
3. Sharing marks or personal details in messages that others could see.
4. Letting the number stay on one staff member's phone.
5. Sending too many messages, which leads parents to block the number.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a school use the same number it already uses on WhatsApp?
Sometimes, but moving an existing number to the API needs planning and may remove it from the normal app. Many institutions choose a new dedicated number.

### Can teachers reply from their own phones?
With a shared inbox, staff reply from a web or mobile app linked to the business number, so their personal numbers are not exposed.

### Can we send report cards on WhatsApp?
Sending a notice with a link to a secure portal is safer than sending the report itself. Discuss privacy with your management and adviser.

### Is WhatsApp better than SMS for schools?
WhatsApp allows richer messages and replies, while SMS reaches every phone. Many schools use WhatsApp for most updates and SMS as a backup for urgent notices.
