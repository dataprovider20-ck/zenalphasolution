Ask five agencies for the price of "a business website" and you may get five very different numbers. That is not always because someone is cheating. It is usually because the five of them are quoting for five different things.

This guide does not give you a single figure, because any honest figure depends on what you need. Instead it explains what moves the price up or down, which costs are often left out of a quote, and how to compare offers fairly. If you can describe your needs clearly, you will get better quotes and fewer surprises.

## Why "how much for a website?" has no single answer

A website can be a one-page profile for a local shop or a store with hundreds of products, payments and delivery tracking. Both are "websites". The work behind them is as different as a tea stall and a restaurant kitchen.

Three things decide most of the price:

1. **How much has to be built** (pages, features, integrations).
2. **How much has to be written and designed from scratch** (content, images, layout).
3. **Who does the work and how** (a freelancer, a small studio or a larger agency, and how much support is included).

## The five factors that decide the price

### 1. The type of website

| Type | What it usually is | Why it costs more or less |
|---|---|---|
| Single-page or profile site | A short page with services and contact details | Least to build and test |
| Business website (5 to 15 pages) | Home, About, Services, Contact, perhaps a blog | The most common starting point for small businesses |
| Lead-generation site | Service pages built to produce enquiries, with forms and tracking | More planning, copywriting and testing |
| Online store | Product catalogue, cart, payments, orders | Payments, stock, shipping and returns add work and testing |
| Custom web application | Logins, dashboards, bookings, workflows | Built to your rules, so it needs design, development and testing |

If you only need people to find you and call, you probably do not need a store or a custom application. Buying more website than your business needs is the most common way to overspend.

### 2. Design: template or custom

A template-based design starts from an existing layout and adapts it to your brand. A custom design is drawn for you from scratch. Templates are faster and cheaper to start with. Custom design suits businesses where the brand or the user journey is a real advantage. Neither is automatically better. A clean, fast, well-written template site often beats an expensive site that nobody can understand.

### 3. Features and integrations

Every extra feature adds build time and testing time. Common ones include:

- Contact and enquiry forms that email you and save to a sheet or CRM
- WhatsApp chat or click-to-call buttons
- Online payments
- Booking or appointment systems
- Multi-language pages
- Login areas for customers
- Connections to other software (CRM, accounting, delivery)

Ask which of these you need on day one and which can wait for a second phase.

### 4. Content: words, photos and videos

Many quotes assume you will provide all the text and images. If you do not have them, someone has to write the copy, source or shoot the photos and prepare product data. That work is real, and it is the part that most often delays a launch. Good words on a plain design usually convert better than weak words on a fancy design.

### 5. Search readiness, speed and security

A site that looks fine can still be slow, hard to find or poorly protected. Check whether the quote covers:

- Mobile layout that works on small screens
- Page speed and image compression
- Basic on-page SEO (titles, descriptions, headings, a sitemap)
- Analytics and search tools set up
- An SSL certificate (the padlock, HTTPS)
- Backups

## The costs that are often missing from a quote

The build price is only part of what you will pay. Ask about these in writing:

- **Domain name.** Renewed every year. Make sure it is registered in your name or your company's name.
- **Hosting.** Where the site lives, and whether the price is yearly or monthly.
- **Business email.** Addresses like info@yourcompany.com are often a separate service.
- **Maintenance and updates.** Who fixes things when a plugin breaks or you want to change a page?
- **Paid plugins, themes or tools.** Some are one-time, some are yearly.
- **Payment gateway fees.** For stores, the gateway usually charges per transaction.
- **Content changes after launch.** Is a number of edits included, or is each one billed?
- **Taxes.** Check whether the quoted figure includes or excludes GST, and confirm the treatment for your business with your accountant.

A low build price with expensive, unclear upkeep can cost more over two years than a clearer package.

## Who builds it: freelancer, studio or agency

| Option | Strengths | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Freelancer | Direct contact, often flexible on price | Single point of failure if they become unavailable |
| Small studio | Design, development and content under one roof | Check who exactly will work on your project |
| Larger agency | Process, project management, a wider team | Can be more than a small business needs |
| Website builder (do it yourself) | Low starting cost, you control the edits | Your time, limited customisation, and the site is only as good as the effort you put in |

None of these is the right answer for everyone. Pick based on your time, your budget and how much you want to manage yourself.

## How to compare quotes fairly

Send every provider the same one-page brief, then compare the answers line by line.

1. **Write the brief first.** Include your business, your customers, the pages you expect, the features you need and examples of sites you like.
2. **Ask for the scope in writing.** Number of pages, design rounds, features, content responsibilities.
3. **Ask what is not included.** This question reveals more than any price.
4. **Ask who owns what.** You should own the domain, the content and the design files on final payment.
5. **Ask about the timeline and the payment schedule.** Staged payments tied to milestones are safer than paying everything upfront.
6. **Ask for relevant work.** Look at live sites they have built and, if possible, speak to a past client.
7. **Ask what happens after launch.** Support period, fixes and how changes are charged.

## Ways to keep the cost sensible without cutting corners

- **Start with the pages that earn enquiries.** Home, services, about and contact usually come first. Add more later.
- **Prepare your content before the project starts.** Waiting for text and photos is a hidden cost in time.
- **Use a clean template where the design is not your edge.** Spend the saved effort on copy and photos.
- **Phase the features.** Launch with the essentials, then add booking, payments or login areas once the basic site is working.
- **Plan for upkeep from the start.** A small, regular maintenance arrangement is cheaper than an emergency fix.

## Red flags in a quote

- No written scope, only a total price
- A promise of "number one on Google" or a guaranteed ranking
- No mention of who owns the domain and files
- Pressure to pay everything immediately
- Refusal to show any live work
- An unusually low price with vague hosting and maintenance terms
- No mention of mobile layout, speed or security

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I get a good business website at a low price?
Often, yes, if your needs are simple: a handful of pages, a clear message and a working contact path. Price tends to rise with custom design, extra features and content creation, so a short, focused scope keeps costs down.

### Is a website builder enough for a small business?
It can be, for a simple site, if you have the time to set it up and keep it updated. The trade-off is less flexibility and your own time. If speed, search visibility or custom features matter, a developer-built site may suit you better.

### Do I have to pay every year?
Usually some costs repeat, such as the domain name and hosting. Ask for these in writing, along with the renewal amounts and who manages them.

### Will a new website bring customers by itself?
No. A website is a place for customers to land. People still need to find it through search, your Business Profile, referrals, messages or ads. Plan how visitors will arrive.

### How long does it take to build a business website?
It depends on the size, the number of review rounds and how quickly you provide content. Ask for a timeline with milestones, and note that late content is the most common cause of delays.

### Should the website be in my name?
Yes. The domain, hosting account and website files should belong to you or your company, and you should hold the login details.
