Front desks at Indian clinics and hospitals spend hours on the phone: booking slots, confirming times, telling patients their reports are ready and answering the same questions about timings and directions. Patients, meanwhile, would often rather send a quick WhatsApp message than wait on hold.

The WhatsApp Business API can take much of this load, as long as it is set up with the sensitivity that health information deserves.

## Where it helps most

- **Appointment confirmations** with date, time, doctor and location.
- **Reminders** the day before, which can reduce no-shows.
- **Report-ready notices** with a link to a secure portal.
- **Directions, timings and preparation instructions**, such as fasting before a blood test.
- **Follow-up reminders** for check-ups or vaccinations.
- **A shared inbox** so reception, billing and the lab can answer their own questions.

## What to be careful about

Health information is sensitive. A few simple rules reduce risk:

1. **Get consent first.** Ask patients at registration whether they agree to receive updates on WhatsApp, and record their answer.
2. **Keep details minimal.** "Your report is ready. View it securely at..." is safer than sending results in the message.
3. **Do not use public chatbots for diagnosis.** Automated replies should handle logistics, not medical advice.
4. **Control access.** Only the staff who need it should see patient chats.
5. **Follow the law.** India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is being phased in and applies to personal data; ask your adviser how it affects your practice.

## A sample patient journey

| Step | Message |
|---|---|
| Booking | "Your appointment with Dr Sharma is confirmed for Tuesday, 11:30 am." |
| Reminder | "Reminder: your appointment is tomorrow at 11:30 am. Reply 1 to confirm or 2 to reschedule." |
| Visit | Directions and parking information on request. |
| Report | "Your test report is ready. View it securely at [portal link]." |
| Follow-up | "It is time for your follow-up visit. Reply to book a slot." |

## Choosing message categories

Appointment confirmations and reminders are usually **utility** messages. Health camp invitations or package offers are **marketing** messages and need appropriate consent. Keep them separate.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can we send lab reports as PDFs on WhatsApp?
It is technically possible, but a secure portal link is usually safer. Decide with your management and adviser, and always have patient consent.

### Can patients book appointments through WhatsApp?
Yes. A simple flow can collect the preferred date and doctor and pass the request to reception or your booking system.

### Will a chatbot give medical advice?
It should not. Use automation for scheduling, directions and FAQs, and route anything medical to qualified staff.

### What if a patient messages at night?
An automatic reply can share emergency contacts and office hours, so patients know when they will get a response.
