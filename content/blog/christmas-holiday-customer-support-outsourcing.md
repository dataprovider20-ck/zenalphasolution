For many online shops and service businesses in the UK and US, the weeks between Black Friday and early January bring more customer messages than any other time of year. Orders go up, delivery questions multiply, gift buyers want reassurance, and in January the returns arrive.

Your in-house team can usually handle a normal week. The holiday peak is different. Response times stretch, the inbox fills overnight, and the customers who wait longest are often the ones most likely to leave a bad review or cancel an order.

Outsourcing part of your customer support for the season is one way to handle this without hiring permanent staff. This guide covers what to hand over, how to brief an outside team quickly, and how to keep quality where you want it.

## What usually goes wrong at Christmas

Most holiday support problems come from a few predictable places:

- **"Where is my order?" messages** rise sharply once delivery cut-off dates approach.
- **After-hours enquiries** pile up overnight and at weekends, so the team starts every morning behind.
- **Repeated simple questions** about delivery dates, gift receipts, stock and discount codes take time away from complex cases.
- **Returns and exchanges** spike after Christmas, just as staff take their own holidays.

None of these needs your most experienced people. They need fast, accurate, polite answers, which makes them good candidates for a well-briefed outside team.

## What to outsource and what to keep

A sensible split is to outsource volume and keep judgement.

**Good to outsource:**

- Order status and delivery date questions
- Answers about returns policy, gift receipts and opening hours
- First response on email and live chat, with clear escalation rules
- Overnight and weekend coverage
- Logging issues and tagging tickets so your team can act on them

**Better to keep in-house:**

- Refunds or compensation above a set amount
- Complaints that may escalate publicly
- Anything involving payment details or account security
- Decisions that need product or policy judgement

Writing this split down before the season starts is half the work. It tells the outside team exactly where their authority ends.

## Use time zones to your advantage

If your business is in the UK or US, a support team in India works while you sleep. That means overnight emails and chats can be answered before your own team logs in, and the morning backlog shrinks.

This works best when the overnight team has clear limits: what they can resolve themselves, what they must hand over, and how to hand it over so your team can pick it up without reading the whole thread again.

## How to brief a team quickly

If you are starting late, the brief matters more than anything else. A good brief can be put together in a few days if you focus on what agents will actually be asked.

1. **Collect your top questions.** Look at the last few weeks of tickets and last year's holiday period. Most businesses find that a small number of question types make up the bulk of messages.
2. **Write approved answers.** Short, clear replies for each common question, in your brand's tone. Agents can adapt them, but they start from something correct.
3. **Share the key dates.** Last order dates for Christmas delivery, holiday opening hours, when returns open, when your warehouse closes.
4. **Set escalation rules.** Who handles what, how quickly, and through which channel.
5. **Give the right access, and no more.** Agents usually need your helpdesk and order lookup. They rarely need full admin rights or payment systems.
6. **Run a short trial.** Have the team answer a batch of real past tickets and review the replies before they go live.

## Protecting quality and your brand

Customers do not care who answers their message. They care whether the answer is correct and friendly. A few habits keep quality steady:

- **Review a sample of conversations daily** during the first week, then weekly.
- **Track a small set of measures:** first response time, resolution time, customer satisfaction and the share of tickets escalated.
- **Hold a quick daily check-in** during the peak weeks to share new issues, such as a delayed courier or a product that has sold out.
- **Update the approved answers** as things change. A delivery cut-off that passed yesterday should not still be in today's replies.

## Data and security basics

Holiday support often involves names, addresses and order details. Treat this carefully:

- Agree in writing how customer data will be handled, stored and deleted.
- Give each agent their own login so actions can be traced.
- Keep payment card details out of chat and email. Refunds should go through your own systems.
- Remove access when the season ends.

If you sell to customers in the UK or EU, make sure your arrangement fits your data protection obligations. Your own legal or compliance adviser should confirm the details for your business.

## After Christmas: plan for returns

The busiest support period is not always before Christmas. Gift returns, exchanges and "the size is wrong" messages often arrive in the first weeks of January. If you are setting up holiday support, include January in the plan rather than ending the arrangement on 25 December.

## How pricing usually works

Seasonal support is usually priced by agent hour, by dedicated agent per week or month, or by ticket volume. Which model suits you depends on how predictable your volume is. Ask any provider to state clearly what is included, the minimum commitment, the notice period and how quickly they can add or remove agents.

## Starting late is still worth it

If Christmas is only a few weeks away, you will not build a perfect operation. You can still take pressure off your team by handing over the simplest, most common questions and overnight coverage first, then adding more as the outside team learns your business.
