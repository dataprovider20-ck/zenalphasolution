Most small businesses do not have a marketing problem. They have a sequencing problem. A website goes up, a Facebook page is created, a few posts go out, someone mentions SEO, someone else mentions ads, and three months later nobody can say what is working.

This guide gives you a simple order of work for the first 90 days. It is written for owners and small teams in the UK, the US, India and anywhere else who do not have a marketing department. It will not promise a number of leads, because that depends on your offer, your market and your budget. What it will give you is a plan short enough to fit on one page and clear enough to review every month.

## What a digital marketing plan actually is

A plan is not a long document. For a small business it is five decisions written down:

1. **The win.** The one action that counts as success (an enquiry, a phone call, a booking, a sale).
2. **The audience.** Who you are trying to reach, in a sentence a stranger would understand.
3. **The offer.** What you sell, to whom, and why they should pick you over the alternative.
4. **Two channels.** Where you will spend your time and money for the next 90 days.
5. **The numbers.** Three or four figures you will look at every week.

If you cannot write each of those in one or two lines, adding another channel will not help. It will only add noise.

## Week 0: decide what a win is

Before you touch a tool, write down the single outcome the marketing is for. "More visibility" is not an outcome. "Ten enquiries a month from people who fit our service" is.

| Type of business | A realistic win | First number to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Local service (clinic, salon, repair) | A phone call or booking request | Calls and booking requests per week |
| B2B service or agency | A qualified enquiry or discovery call | Enquiries and calls booked per month |
| Online shop | A completed order | Orders and conversion rate |
| Software or SaaS | A trial sign-up or demo request | Sign-ups per week |
| Education or coaching | A form submission or enrolment call | Applications per month |

Pick one. You can add a second later. Teams that track four wins at once usually end up tracking none.

## Days 1 to 30: fix the foundations before you spend anything

Traffic sent to a weak website is wasted traffic, whether it came from search, social media or ads. The first month is about making sure that when someone arrives, they understand you and can contact you easily.

### 1. Run a three-second test on your homepage

Show your homepage to someone who has never seen it for three seconds, then ask what the business does and who it is for. If they cannot answer, rewrite the headline and the line below it. Say the service and the customer plainly, for example "Accounting for freelancers in Manchester" rather than "Empowering financial success".

### 2. Give every page one obvious next step

A page with five buttons usually gets none of them pressed. Decide the one action for each page (call, book, request a quote) and make it the most visible element. Secondary links can sit lower.

### 3. Test your own contact path

Fill in your contact form from your phone. Does the confirmation appear? Does the email actually arrive, and where? Click your WhatsApp or call button. This takes ten minutes and is the most common thing small businesses discover is broken only after they have paid for traffic.

### 4. Check speed and layout on a phone

Open the site on mobile data, not office Wi-Fi. If a page takes a long time to load, or text and buttons are hard to read, fix that before anything else. Large uncompressed images are the usual cause, and they are cheap to fix.

### 5. Set up measurement

You cannot improve what you cannot see. Free tools cover the basics:

- **Google Search Console** shows which searches show your site and which pages Google has indexed.
- **Google Analytics 4** shows where visitors come from and what they do. Mark your win (a form submission, a call click) as a key event so you can count it.
- **Microsoft Clarity** or a similar tool records anonymous sessions, so you can watch where visitors hesitate.

If you serve visitors in the UK or EU, make sure your cookie notice and privacy policy match what these tools actually do.

### 6. Claim the free local listings that apply

If customers find you by searching "near me" or by area, set up a Google Business Profile and keep your name, address (or service area), phone and hours identical everywhere. Our guide to [setting up a Google Business Profile](google-business-profile-for-small-business-india.html) covers the common mistakes.

### 7. Keep a simple record of enquiries

A spreadsheet is enough: date, name, source, what they wanted, status. Without it you cannot tell which channel produced customers, only which produced clicks.

## Days 31 to 60: choose two channels, not seven

This is where most plans go wrong. Every channel looks attractive and every agency sells a different one. Choose two, run them properly for a full month and judge them on your win, not on likes or followers.

Three questions make the choice easier:

- **Where are your customers already looking?** A plumber is found through local search and referrals, not TikTok. A software consultancy is found through LinkedIn conversations and search for specific problems.
- **How quickly do you need results?** Paid search, direct outreach and email to existing contacts can produce conversations in days or weeks. SEO usually takes months to build.
- **How long is your sales cycle?** A takeaway meal is decided in minutes. A website rebuild takes weeks of comparing options, so content that educates matters more.

Here is a plain comparison to help you choose.

| Channel | Works best when | What you do in month two | The signal to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Search engine optimisation (SEO) | People already search for what you sell | Fix technical issues, write pages that answer specific questions, build internal links | Impressions and clicks in Search Console, then enquiries |
| Local search (Business Profile) | You serve a local area | Complete the profile, add photos, request genuine reviews, answer questions | Calls, direction requests, review count |
| Email | You already have contacts or customers | Send a useful message on a regular schedule to people who agreed to hear from you | Replies and enquiries, not just opens |
| WhatsApp or SMS (opt-in only) | You have customers who want updates, bookings or reminders | Send relevant, permission-based messages with an easy way to stop | Replies, bookings, opt-outs |
| LinkedIn outreach | You sell to businesses | Publish useful posts, send a small number of personal connection requests and follow up | Conversations started, calls booked |
| Paid search | People search with buying intent and you can afford to test | Start with a small test budget on a few specific searches and a tight landing page | Cost per enquiry, and whether the enquiries are any good |
| Organic social media | You have visual or community-led content | Post consistently and reply to every comment | Messages and enquiries, not follower count |
| Referrals and partnerships | Your work is trusted by word of mouth | Ask satisfied customers and partners directly, make referring easy | Referred enquiries per month |

A good starting pair for many small service businesses is one channel that brings quick conversations (direct outreach, email to existing contacts or a small paid-search test) and one that compounds slowly (SEO and a complete Business Profile).

## Days 61 to 90: improve what you have, do not add more

By now you have a month or two of numbers. Use them.

- **Find the biggest leak.** Are people not arriving, arriving and leaving, or enquiring and not buying? Each needs a different fix. Few visitors points to channel and content problems. Visitors without enquiries point to the page or the offer. Enquiries without customers point to follow-up speed, price or fit.
- **Double down on the one thing that produced real conversations.** If a certain blog post, a certain message or a certain referral partner produced enquiries, do more of that before you try something new.
- **Stop what produced nothing.** Be honest. A channel that took a month of real effort and produced no enquiries is information, not failure.

## A weekly rhythm you can actually keep

Consistency beats intensity. A small team can run the plan with a short weekly routine like this one (adjust the time to your situation):

- **Monday, 30 minutes:** look at your numbers (visits by source, enquiries, calls) and note one thing that changed.
- **Tuesday and Wednesday:** work on channel one (for example, write or improve one page, or send your outreach messages).
- **Thursday:** work on channel two (for example, request reviews, update your profile or send an email).
- **Friday, 30 minutes:** reply to every open enquiry, and update your enquiry spreadsheet.

Notice what is missing: scrolling social media "for inspiration" and chasing every new tool. The rhythm is deliberately boring.

## How to think about budget

There is no honest universal percentage, because it depends on your margins and how fast you want results. A more useful way to think about it:

1. **Time is the first budget.** Most of the early work (the foundations, the enquiry record, outreach, writing) costs hours, not money.
2. **Tools can stay free or cheap at the start.** The measurement tools above cost nothing.
3. **Pay only for what you can measure.** If you cannot count the enquiries from a paid activity, do not pay for it yet.
4. **Set a test budget you can afford to lose.** Agree the amount, the time window and what result would make you continue, before you start.
5. **Never put paid traffic on a broken page.** Fix the contact path first.

## The numbers that matter (and the ones that do not)

Track these:

- Visits by source (search, direct, social, email, referral)
- Number of enquiries, and where each came from
- Enquiry-to-customer rate
- Time to first reply to an enquiry
- Cost per enquiry, if you spend on ads
- Repeat customers or referrals

Treat these with caution: follower counts, impressions on their own, and "engagement" that never turns into a conversation. They can be useful context, but they are not the win.

## Two illustrative plans

These are examples of how the framework looks on one page. They are not case studies or results.

**Example 1: a local dental clinic**
- Win: new-patient booking requests.
- Channel one: complete Business Profile, a steady flow of genuine patient reviews, accurate hours and services.
- Channel two: a small set of service pages (for example, "teeth whitening in [area]") that answer the questions patients ask.
- Weekly numbers: calls, booking requests, new reviews.

**Example 2: a small B2B software consultancy**
- Win: a discovery call with a qualified founder or manager.
- Channel one: LinkedIn, with two useful posts a week and a handful of personal connection requests with a short follow-up.
- Channel two: SEO around specific problems the consultancy solves, with each page ending in a clear next step.
- Weekly numbers: conversations started, calls booked, enquiries from search.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Starting channels before the website can convert.
- Choosing channels because competitors use them, not because customers do.
- Running five channels at low effort instead of two properly.
- Measuring followers and impressions instead of enquiries.
- Sending messages to people who never agreed to hear from you.
- Changing the plan every week, so no result can be read.
- Replying to enquiries slowly. A fast, helpful reply often beats a slightly better offer.
- Expecting SEO to behave like ads. SEO takes time and compounds. Ads switch on and off.

## How long until you see results?

It depends on the channel. Activities that start conversations directly, such as personal outreach, email to existing contacts or a carefully targeted paid test, can produce replies within days or weeks. Search engine optimisation usually takes several months before it brings steady enquiries, and nobody can promise a ranking. A realistic way to treat the first 90 days is as the period in which you fix the foundations, learn what your customers respond to and decide where to invest next.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should a small business spend on digital marketing?
There is no single correct figure. Start with the time you can commit, free measurement tools and a small test budget you can afford to lose. Increase spend only on activities where you can see enquiries coming from them.

### Do I need to be on every social media platform?
No. Choose the one or two platforms where your customers actually spend time and where you can post consistently. A well-kept profile on one platform is better than five neglected ones.

### Should I start with SEO or paid ads?
If you need conversations quickly and have a clear offer and a good landing page, a small paid test or direct outreach can start sooner. SEO is slower but builds assets that keep working. Many small businesses use one of each, as long as the website is ready first.

### How long does SEO take to work?
Usually several months for a new website, and it depends on competition, content quality and the technical health of the site. Be wary of anyone guaranteeing a ranking or a timeline.

### What is the first number I should track?
Count enquiries, and where each one came from. If you only track one thing, make it that.

### Can I do this myself without an agency?
Often, yes, at the start. The foundations, the enquiry record and the weekly rhythm do not need an agency. Specialists become useful when you have a clear goal and a specific gap, such as technical SEO, a messaging setup or a website rebuild.

### How often should I review the plan?
Look at the numbers weekly and review the plan once a month. Change one thing at a time so you can tell what made the difference.
