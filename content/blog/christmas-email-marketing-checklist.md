Christmas is the season when almost every business sends more email than usual. That is exactly why it is also the season when many campaigns go to spam, get ignored or annoy people into unsubscribing. Inboxes are crowded, mailbox providers are watching volume closely, and customers are quick to delete anything that does not feel relevant.

This checklist covers what to do before, during and after the holiday season so your emails have a fair chance of being seen and acted on.

## Before you send anything

### 1. Check your domain authentication

If SPF, DKIM and DMARC are missing or broken, the rest of this list will not help much. Send a test email to a Gmail address, open it, click "Show original" and confirm all three pass. If you are not sure what these records do, our guide on [SPF, DKIM and DMARC](spf-dkim-dmarc-why-bulk-emails-go-to-spam.html) explains them in plain language.

### 2. Clean your list

Remove addresses that have bounced and contacts who have not opened or clicked anything in a long time. It feels wrong to send to fewer people at the busiest time of year, but sending to inactive addresses lowers engagement and can push your whole campaign towards spam.

### 3. Do not suddenly send far more than usual

Mailbox providers notice when a sender who normally sends a few thousand emails a month suddenly sends many times that. If you plan to increase volume for Christmas, start increasing it gradually a few weeks before your main campaigns, beginning with your most engaged contacts.

### 4. Never add bought or scraped lists

The holiday season tempts businesses to grow their list fast. Purchased lists bring complaints, spam traps and bounces. The short-term reach is not worth the long-term damage to your domain.

## Planning the campaign

### 5. Map your key dates

Write down the dates that matter for your customers:

- Black Friday and Cyber Monday offers
- Your last order date for Christmas delivery, by region
- Last date for express delivery
- Holiday opening hours or support hours
- Boxing Day and January sale start dates
- When returns and exchanges open after Christmas

These dates give each email a real reason to exist. "Last day to order for Christmas delivery" is useful. A tenth generic "Holiday deals" email is not.

### 6. Segment instead of blasting

Split your list so people get emails that fit them:

- Recent buyers versus people who have not bought yet
- UK versus US customers, because delivery dates and time zones differ
- High-value customers who might want early access
- Customers who bought gifts last year

Even simple segments usually perform better than sending one message to everyone.

### 7. Plan your frequency

Decide in advance how many emails each segment will get and stick to it. Sending daily for a month to your whole list is a common way to lose subscribers. Let engaged customers receive more and give everyone an easy way to reduce frequency or unsubscribe.

## Writing the emails

### 8. Subject lines that are clear, not loud

Say what the email is about. "Last day for Christmas delivery in the UK" works better than "HUGE XMAS SALE!!! DON'T MISS OUT". Avoid all capitals, piles of exclamation marks and misleading "Re:" or "Fwd:" tricks.

### 9. Design for mobile first

Many people read holiday emails on their phones while doing something else. Use a single column, readable text, one main button and images that still make sense if they do not load.

### 10. Keep the unsubscribe link visible

A clear unsubscribe link protects you. People who cannot find it press "Report spam" instead, and complaints hurt far more than unsubscribes.

### 11. Include a plain-text version

Most email platforms create one automatically. Check that it reads properly and that links work.

## Sending and timing

### 12. Respect time zones

If you sell in both the UK and the US, schedule separate sends so each audience receives the email at a sensible local time. An email that lands at 3 a.m. sits under everything else by morning.

### 13. Send a test to yourself and colleagues

Check the email on a phone and a computer, in Gmail and Outlook, and click every link. Make sure discount codes actually work before thousands of people try them.

### 14. Watch results for the first hour

Keep an eye on bounces, complaints and unsubscribes after a send. A sudden jump in complaints means something about the list or the message needs fixing before the next email goes out.

## After Christmas

### 15. Plan January emails now

The season does not end on 25 December. Useful post-Christmas emails include:

- How to return or exchange a gift
- Boxing Day or January sale details
- A thank-you to customers who bought, with no hard sell
- Setup tips or guides for products people received

These emails often get good engagement because they help people with something they are doing anyway.

### 16. Review what worked

Note which subject lines, segments and send times performed best, and which emails caused unsubscribes. That record makes next year's planning much easier.
