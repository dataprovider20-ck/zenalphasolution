Every December, people search differently. They look for "open on Christmas Eve", "last order date for Christmas delivery", "gift vouchers near me" and "Boxing Day sale". Many of those searches happen on a phone, in a hurry, a few miles from where they will spend their money.

Large retailers plan for this months ahead. Small businesses often do not, and they lose sales to competitors whose Google listings simply show the right opening hours.

This checklist is written for small and mid-sized businesses in the UK, the US and Ireland. Most of it costs nothing except an hour or two of work, and much of it still helps if you start in December.

## Why Christmas search is different

Three things change during the holiday season.

**Searches become more local and more urgent.** People want to know who is open, who can deliver in time and who is nearby. A missing or wrong detail on your listing can send them straight to someone else.

**Searches include dates and deadlines.** Phrases like "before Christmas", "order by" and "open today" appear far more often. Pages that answer those questions clearly have an advantage.

**The calendar differs by country.** In the US, the season starts with Thanksgiving weekend, Black Friday and Cyber Monday. In the UK the big moments are Black Friday, the last posting dates and Boxing Day. In Ireland, 26 December is St Stephen's Day, and many businesses close or run sales around it. If you sell into more than one of these markets, your pages and hours should reflect each one.

## Step 1: Set your holiday hours on Google and Bing

This is the single fastest win, and it is free.

In your Google Business Profile, use the **special hours** option to set opening times for Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, Boxing Day or St Stephen's Day, New Year's Eve and New Year's Day. Do the same in Bing Places. If you are closed, say so. A customer who travels to a closed shop rarely forgives it, and they may leave a review that says so.

While you are there:

- Check that your phone number is answered during the hours you list.
- Add a short post about your Christmas offer, delivery cut-off or opening times.
- Add a few recent, real photos. Seasonal photos of your shop or work are fine.

If you have not set up a profile yet, our guide to [setting up a Google Business Profile](google-business-profile-for-small-business-india.html) walks through it step by step. The steps are the same in every country.

## Step 2: Build one seasonal landing page, and keep its address

Create a single page for your Christmas offer: gift ideas, holiday services, opening times and delivery dates. Give it a simple, permanent web address such as `/christmas` rather than `/christmas-2026`.

The reason is simple. A page that stays at the same address year after year keeps the links and history it has built. Next November you update the content instead of starting again from zero. Between seasons, you can keep the page live with a short note about when the next offer starts.

A good seasonal page includes:

- A clear headline that matches what people search for, such as "Christmas gift vouchers in Dublin" or "Christmas catering in Manchester".
- Prices or "from" prices where you can.
- Delivery and collection cut-off dates for each country you serve.
- Opening hours over the holidays.
- One obvious action: call, book, order or message.

## Step 3: Answer the questions people actually type

Look at the questions your customers ask every December and answer them on your site in plain words. Typical ones are:

- What is the last day to order for Christmas delivery to the UK, the US or Ireland?
- Are you open on Christmas Eve, Boxing Day or St Stephen's Day?
- Do you sell gift vouchers, and can they be sent by email?
- What is your returns policy for Christmas gifts?

A short FAQ section on your Christmas page often appears for exactly these searches. Keep the answers short and specific, and update them as dates pass.

## Step 4: Make the page fast on a phone

Holiday shoppers are impatient, and most of them are on mobile. Before the busiest weeks:

- Open your Christmas page on your own phone using mobile data, not Wi-Fi.
- Check that the main button is visible without scrolling.
- Compress large images. A banner that looks beautiful but takes several seconds to load is costing you visitors.
- Test your contact form and checkout end to end.

## Step 5: Use the right words for each country

Small differences in wording affect what people find:

- "Holiday" is common in the US. "Christmas" and "festive" are more common in the UK and Ireland.
- "Boxing Day" is a UK term. In Ireland, use "St Stephen's Day" as well.
- Mention prices in the currency your customer uses: pounds, dollars or euros.
- If you serve specific towns or cities, name them naturally in your text, not as a list stuffed into the page.

Do not create dozens of near-identical pages for different cities with only the place name changed. Search engines treat those as low-quality pages, and they can drag down the rest of your site. One strong page per real offer works better.

## Step 6: Link your channels together

Your Christmas page works harder when other channels point to it:

- Link to it from your homepage during the season.
- Mention it in your Christmas emails. Our [Christmas email marketing checklist](christmas-email-marketing-checklist.html) covers how to send those without landing in spam.
- Share it on LinkedIn, Facebook and Instagram, and pin it where you can.
- Add it to your Google Business Profile post.

Each link sends visitors and tells search engines the page matters.

## Step 7: Track what happens

You cannot improve what you do not measure. At minimum:

- In **Google Search Console**, check which search terms bring impressions and clicks to your Christmas page.
- In **Google Analytics**, track enquiries, calls and WhatsApp clicks, not just visits.
- Note your best and worst days. That record makes next year's planning much easier.

## After Christmas: do not switch everything off

The days after Christmas are busy in their own way. People search for returns, exchanges, January sales and "how to use" guides for gifts they received. Update your seasonal page with:

- Returns and exchange details.
- January sale dates.
- A short thank-you message and what is coming next.

Then, in January, write down what worked. Which page got the most visits? Which search terms appeared? Which day brought the most enquiries? Those notes are the start of next year's plan.

## A realistic word on timing

If you start in December, you will not overtake large retailers for broad searches like "Christmas gifts". You can still win local, specific searches, the ones from people ready to buy nearby, by getting your hours, your page and your answers right. And everything you build this year keeps working next November.
