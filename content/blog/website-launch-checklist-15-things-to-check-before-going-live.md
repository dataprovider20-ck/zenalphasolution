A new website feels finished the moment the design looks good. That is usually when the real checking should begin. Most launch-day problems are not about colours or fonts. They are broken forms, pages that Google cannot index, images that make mobile visitors wait, and links that lead nowhere.



This website launch checklist covers 15 things we look at before a business site goes live. You can use it for a new website, a redesign, or a final review of a site you already own. Go through it slowly and tick each point.



\## Part 1: Content and structure



\### 1. One focused page for each service



If you sell five services, a single long home page will rarely rank for all five. Create a separate page for each service, with its own heading, explanation, questions and enquiry button. A visitor searching for one thing should land on a page about exactly that thing.



\### 2. A clear heading on every page



Each page needs one main heading (H1) that says what the page is about, followed by smaller headings that break up the content. This helps readers scan, and it helps search engines understand the topic.



\### 3. Content that answers real questions



Write for the questions your customers actually ask on calls and in messages: what does it cost to start, how long does it take, what do I need to give you. A short FAQ section on each service page is a simple way to cover them. Avoid copying the same paragraph across many pages, because duplicate text adds little value and can hold pages back in search.



\### 4. Honest claims only



Do not publish numbers, awards or client names that you cannot support. If you say you have delivered a certain number of projects, be ready to show it. Show logos only with the client's permission. Trust is built by small, true details.



\## Part 2: Search engine basics



\### 5. Unique title and description for every page



The title tag is the blue headline people see in search results. Keep it under about 60 characters and put the main topic near the start. The meta description is the short text under it. Aim for roughly 120 to 160 characters and write it like a sentence a person would want to click on.



\### 6. Clean, readable URLs



A URL such as /website-development is better than /page?id=27. Keep them short, lowercase and descriptive. Decide on the format before launch, because changing URLs later needs redirects.



\### 7. A sitemap and a robots file



A sitemap lists the pages you want search engines to find. A robots file tells crawlers what to skip. Before launch, make sure the robots file is not blocking the whole site by mistake. This is more common than you might think, especially if the site was built on a testing address.



\### 8. Canonical tags and no accidental noindex



A canonical tag tells Google which address is the main version of a page. A noindex tag tells Google to leave a page out of search. Check that your important pages are indexable, and that only thin or private pages carry noindex.



\### 9. Connect Google Search Console



After launch, add your site to Google Search Console, submit the sitemap and check the Pages report after a week or two. It will show which pages are indexed and which have problems. It is free and it is the most honest view of how Google sees your site.



\## Part 3: Speed and mobile



\### 10. Test on a real phone



Most visitors will open your site on a mobile. Do not only resize a desktop browser window. Pick up a phone and test the menu, the buttons, the forms and the text size. Check that nothing overlaps and that the tap targets are large enough.



\### 11. Compress your images



Large photos are the most common reason a business website feels slow. Resize images to the size they are actually displayed, use a compressed format, and add a short description (alt text) so that the image also makes sense to screen readers and search engines.



\### 12. Remove what you do not need



Every script, plugin and font adds loading time. Keep only what the site really uses. A lighter website loads faster, breaks less often and is easier to maintain.



\## Part 4: Forms, tracking and security



\### 13. Test every form



Fill in each enquiry, contact and job application form yourself. Confirm that the message arrives in the right inbox, that the confirmation message appears, and that a file upload works if there is one. Also check your spam folder, since the first notifications from a new site are sometimes filtered. Use a business email address on the site rather than a personal one, so customers see a name they can trust.



\### 14. Set up analytics



Install an analytics tool before launch, not after, so you do not lose the first weeks of data. Decide what counts as a success: a form submission, a phone call click, a WhatsApp chat. If you do not measure it, you cannot improve it. If you have visitors from Europe or other regions with privacy laws, add a clear privacy policy and follow the consent rules that apply to you.



\### 15. HTTPS, backups and a 404 page



Your site should open on a secure HTTPS address with no browser warnings. Keep a backup of the site files so you can restore them quickly. Create a friendly 404 page that guides lost visitors back to your main pages instead of showing a blank error.



\## After launch: the first 30 days



Launching is the start of the work. In the first month:



\- Check Search Console for errors and see which pages are indexed.

\- Review your analytics to find the pages people visit and where they leave.

\- Fix broken links and slow pages as you find them.

\- Publish helpful content regularly, such as a new guide or an answer to a common customer question.

\- Ask satisfied customers for genuine reviews and testimonials.



Search results take time to build, and nobody can honestly promise a ranking on a certain date. A well-built site, useful content and steady improvement are what produce results over the months that follow.
