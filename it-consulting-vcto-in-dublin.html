Virtual CTO Services for Dublin Startups and SMEs

Part-time technical leadership for Irish founders and growing companies: architecture decisions, supplier oversight and investor-ready documentation, from a team in New Delhi.

Why Irish Founders Bring In a Part-Time CTO

Many Dublin start-ups are built by commercial founders who know their market well but have never chosen a cloud platform or managed developers. They are often funded in stages, so committing to a senior technical salary too early would burn runway that should go into the product.

A virtual CTO sits between those two positions. We help you set the technical direction, check what your contractors deliver and prepare the technical story that investors and enterprise customers will probe.

  • A written architecture review in plain English.
  • Independent checks on agency quotes and progress.
  • Technical sections for funding or grant applications.
  • A security baseline before your first large customer.
  • Interview questions for your first in-house engineer.

Irish and EU Points We Plan Around

Ireland's Data Protection Commission acts as lead regulator for many technology companies with European headquarters in Dublin, which keeps privacy on the agenda for buyers here. We design with GDPR in mind and document where data lives. If you claim the Irish R&D tax credit, contemporaneous technical records help your accountant; whether work qualifies is their call, not ours.

Our Opening Month

  1. A kick-off call during the Irish morning.
  2. Read-only access to code, cloud billing and supplier contracts.
  3. A findings note ranked by cost and risk.
  4. An agreed monthly rhythm, reviewed each quarter.

What a Virtual CTO Does in the First Thirty Days

A virtual CTO is not a consultant who disappears after a slide deck. For a Dublin company without a full-time technology lead, the first month sets the shape of everything that follows, so it helps to know what to expect.

In the first week we read, we do not change. That means going through the repository, the cloud accounts, the deployment process, the list of vendors and the most recent incidents. We talk to the developers and to whoever pays the bills, because the technical picture and the business picture rarely match at first.

By the end of week two, you get a short written risk list. It names what could hurt you in the next six months, such as a single developer who holds all the knowledge, backups that were never tested, or a contract that auto-renews. Each item has a rough size and a proposed owner.

Weeks three and four turn that into a plan. We agree three or four priorities, decide what to stop doing and set up a regular call. Many Irish teams sell to customers who ask security questions, so we also draft honest answers about how data is stored and who can access it. After thirty days you should know where you stand, what comes next and who is responsible for each step.

Questions from Dublin clients

How many hours overlap with Irish working days?

India is 5½ hours ahead of Dublin in winter and 4½ in summer, so a normal Irish working day and ours share roughly four to five hours.

Can you help us prepare for investor technical questions?

We can write a clear summary of your architecture, risks and roadmap. We make no promises about funding decisions.

Do you work under GDPR data processing terms?

Yes. We can sign confidentiality and data processing terms before you share any personal data, and we access systems only through accounts you control.

Can the arrangement be stopped easily?

Engagements are agreed month by month after the first review, with notice terms written into the contract.
