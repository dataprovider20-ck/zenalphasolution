AI Consulting for Stockholm Businesses

AI advice for Stockholm fintech, retail and service companies: careful pilots in Swedish and English, with high-risk uses identified before anything is built.

Know the Risk Level Before You Build

Swedish companies adopt digital tools quickly, and AI is no exception. Yet some uses carry far more obligations than others. Under the EU AI Act, systems used to assess the creditworthiness of individuals are classed as high-risk, while a tool that drafts internal summaries is not.

For Stockholm fintech and lending firms this distinction shapes the whole project. We map your intended use against the risk categories first, then design a pilot that delivers value without stumbling into heavy obligations by accident.

  • A short risk classification of each proposed AI use.
  • Swedish and English document summaries for back-office teams.
  • Customer service drafts for human approval.
  • Fraud and anomaly alerts that a person investigates.
  • Reporting assistants for finance teams.

Swedish Language and Data

Swedish is well supported by leading models, but specialised terms in finance or law can trip them up. We build test sets from your own texts. Personal data remains subject to GDPR, enforced by IMY, and we keep sensitive information out of tools that do not need it.

Our Way of Working

  1. Classify the use case and agree boundaries.
  2. Collect a small, representative test set.
  3. Build and evaluate a prototype in Swedish and English.
  4. Recommend scale-up, changes or a stop.

Common questions in Stockholm

Is credit scoring with AI allowed?

It is permitted but treated as high-risk under the EU AI Act, with significant duties. Seek legal advice before building such a system.

How well does AI work in Swedish?

Generally well for everyday business language. We test specialist vocabulary on your own documents.

Can you work with our in-house data team?

Yes. We often act as an outside reviewer and pilot partner for existing teams.

Can we hold workshops in Swedish office hours?

Yes. Swedish mornings fall in our early afternoon, so workshops and reviews fit comfortably.
