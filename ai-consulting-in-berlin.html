AI Consulting for Berlin Companies

Focused AI pilots for Berlin start-ups and established firms, with careful attention to German-language quality, data location and employee consultation.

Getting German Right

A tool that works well in English can sound stiff or wrong in German, especially in formal customer letters where the choice between Sie and du matters. Berlin companies serving German-speaking customers need AI output that reads naturally, or it costs more to correct than to write.

We test language quality first, on your own texts, before we look at automation. Only when a native reviewer is satisfied do we connect the helper to real workflows.

  • German and English drafts for customer emails, reviewed by staff.
  • Summaries of contracts, tenders or application documents.
  • Internal knowledge search across Confluence, drives or wikis.
  • Structured data extracted from invoices and forms.
  • Meeting notes turned into tasks, with consent from participants.

Involving the Right People

Where a company has a works council, software that could monitor employees usually needs its involvement before introduction. German buyers also tend to ask where data is processed. We prepare a short description of each pilot, its data flows and its hosting region, so that the works council, data protection officer and management can decide together.

Pilot Plan

  1. Agree a use case and invite the people affected.
  2. Test German-language quality on real examples.
  3. Run a limited trial with clear on and off switches.
  4. Report results and open questions to decision-makers.

Testing AI Output Before Customers See It

Berlin companies tend to be careful about quality, and rightly so. An AI assistant that is right nine times in ten can still do real damage with the tenth answer, so the way you test it matters as much as the way you build it.

We begin with a test set: a few dozen to a few hundred real examples, each with the answer an expert would give. Before changing anything, run the system against that set and record the score. Every later change, such as a new prompt or a different model, is then checked against the same examples.

Numbers alone do not tell the whole story. Someone from the team should read a sample of the outputs each week. They will notice tone problems, invented details and cases the test set missed. When they find one, it goes into the test set so the same mistake is caught next time.

For customer-facing use, add a human step where the cost of an error is high, and a clear fallback when the system is unsure. Log inputs and outputs so you can investigate a complaint. Under the EU rules on AI, documentation and human oversight matter more for some uses than others, and we help you work out which category your project falls into. A simple, well-tested assistant is more useful than an ambitious one nobody trusts.

What Berlin teams ask us

Is AI output good enough in German?

Often yes, but not always. We measure quality with native reviewers on your own material before recommending use.

Can data stay in the EU?

We can choose providers and hosting regions in the EU when your policies or customers require it.

Do we need to involve our works council?

If the tool could monitor employees, usually yes. We prepare documentation that helps that conversation; your HR or legal adviser confirms the process.
