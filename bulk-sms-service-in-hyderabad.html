Sending SMS in Telugu and Urdu from Hyderabad

Hyderabad businesses speak to customers in several languages, often in the same week. A pharmacy chain may text in Telugu, a clinic in Urdu and a software company in English. Local-language messages build trust, but they behave differently from English ones, and it is better to understand that before you print a single campaign.

A standard English SMS fits 160 characters in one segment. Telugu and Urdu use Unicode, where a single segment holds only 70 characters, and longer messages are split into segments of 67. A message that looks short on screen can quietly turn into three or four billed segments. Write the local-language version first, count the segments, and cut it down before registering the template.

Each language version needs its own approved DLT template. Keep the variable fields, such as a name or an appointment time, short and predictable, because a long value can push a one-segment message into two. Test the message on both Android and iPhone, since some older handsets render certain Telugu characters poorly.

For Hyderabad's pharma, education and IT-services customers, a useful pattern is a short local-language reminder followed by the same details in English for people who prefer it. Send each version only to the contacts who chose that language, rather than doubling every message. If you are not sure which language a contact prefers, ask once on the sign-up form and store the answer.
