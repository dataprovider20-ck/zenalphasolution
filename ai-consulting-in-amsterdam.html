AI for Amsterdam E-commerce and Logistics

Practical AI projects for Amsterdam online retailers and logistics operators: product data, delivery questions and returns, tested against your own numbers.

From Catalogue to Doorstep

The Netherlands is a gateway for goods entering Europe, and Amsterdam is home to many online shops and fulfilment businesses that sell across borders. Their recurring chores are clear: writing and translating product data, answering delivery questions and processing returns.

We pick one of those chores and measure it before changing anything. If an AI helper saves real time without increasing mistakes, we help you roll it out; if not, we say so.

  • Product titles and descriptions drafted in Dutch, English and German.
  • Answers to "where is my parcel" questions using your tracking data.
  • Returns reasons classified automatically for weekly review.
  • Duplicate or missing product data flagged before it goes live.
  • Demand notes for buyers based on recent sales.

Staff, Customers and Data

If an AI tool could monitor how employees perform, larger Dutch companies may need works council consent before using it. Customer data falls under GDPR, overseen by the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. We keep pilots narrow, avoid sending unnecessary personal data and write down what each tool sees.

How We Would Begin

  1. Pick the chore that costs most hours each week.
  2. Measure today's time and error rate.
  3. Build a helper and test it on past orders or products.
  4. Compare results and agree the next step.

Is Your Data Ready for AI? A Checklist for Amsterdam Operations

Many Amsterdam logistics and e-commerce companies have plenty of data and little confidence in it. Before any AI project, it is worth checking whether the data can support what you want to build.

Start with where the data lives. Orders may sit in one system, shipments in another and customer messages in a shared mailbox. If an answer requires joining those, someone has to build and maintain that connection. Next, check quality. Look at a random sample of fifty records and note how many have missing fields, duplicate entries or free-text notes that mean different things to different people.

Then ask about access and permission. Who may see which data, and does the purpose you have in mind match what customers were told when you collected it? If personal data is involved, involve whoever handles privacy at your company early, not at the end.

Finally, check whether there is a record of correct answers. If you want a system to classify returns, you need examples of past returns that were classified properly. Without them you cannot test whether the system works. The result of the checklist is a short report with a status for each point, and an honest view of whether to proceed, fix the data first or choose a smaller project.

Amsterdam: frequently asked

Can AI write product descriptions in Dutch?

Yes, and in other EU languages. We recommend a native speaker checks a sample before anything goes live.

Does it connect to our shop platform?

Usually through exports or APIs. We check your platform before promising an integration.

Will it replace our customer service team?

The goal is to answer simple repeated questions faster so that your team can focus on harder cases.
