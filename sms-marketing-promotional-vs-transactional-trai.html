SMS Marketing in India: Promotional vs. Transactional SMS and TRAI Rules

Published 1 November 2026 · 4 min read · By Zen Alpha Solution

Get a Free Consultation

Despite the rise of messaging apps and social media, the traditional SMS is still one of the most direct ways for a business to reach customers. While emails sit unread in crowded inboxes and social posts get buried by algorithms, a text message appears right on the phone screen. But running a bulk SMS campaign in India is no longer a free-for-all. To protect people from spam and phishing, the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) enforces strict rules built on Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT). Whether you are sending your first discount code or real-time banking alerts, you need to understand the difference between SMS types.

The Core Divide: Promotional vs. Transactional SMS

Before you launch any campaign, decide what kind of message you are sending. Using the wrong route can lead to blocked messages, penalties and even the blacklisting of your sender ID.

Transactional SMS

Transactional messages are informational. They are triggered by a customer's own action or are needed for a service the customer already uses. Because they carry important information, they can reach customers even if Do Not Disturb (DND) is active on the phone, and they are not limited to business hours. Common examples include:

  • Authentication: one-time passwords (OTPs) for login or payment verification.
  • Alerts: account debit and credit alerts or booking changes.
  • Confirmations: order confirmations, shipping updates and digital receipts.
  • Ticketing: booking confirmations and event registrations.

Promotional SMS

Promotional messages aim to drive sales or brand awareness. If a message includes an offer, a discount or an upsell, treat it as promotional. TRAI restricts this route to protect consumers: promotional messages are not delivered to numbers that have opted out through DND, and they may be sent only within a fixed daytime and evening window. Providers currently cite 10 AM to 9 PM, while earlier rules said 9 AM to 9 PM, so confirm the current window with your provider. Examples include:

  • Retail offers: a weekend sale on winter wear.
  • Property marketing: an invitation to a new project site visit.
  • Event marketing: a reminder to register for your webinar.

Service Messages

Between the two sit service messages, such as delivery tracking and appointment updates for existing customers. They follow their own rules. TRAI updated its regulations in 2025, including changes to message categories and a suffix system for sender headers, so always check the latest rules with your SMS provider before you plan campaigns.

TRAI's DLT Framework in Plain Words

Indian mobile users were once flooded with unwanted commercial messages. In response, TRAI introduced its Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations in 2018 and brought in a blockchain-based DLT system. Today every business, sender ID and message template must be registered on the operators' platform before a single message is sent. You can read the regulations on the TRAI website. Headers also follow a pattern: transactional senders usually use six letters, while promotional senders use six digits.

Step-by-Step DLT Compliance for Businesses

  1. Principal Entity registration: register your business on a telecom operator's DLT portal, such as those run by Jio, Airtel, Vi or BSNL. You typically submit KYC documents like a PAN, a GST certificate and an authorisation letter, though requirements can vary. You then receive an Entity ID.
  2. Header (sender ID) registration: register a six-character header linked to your business name. For Zen Alpha Solution, a header might be something like ZENALP, subject to approval.
  3. Template registration: every message must be approved in advance. You submit the exact text and replace changing details, such as names, dates or OTPs, with a {#var#} placeholder.
  4. Consent records: for promotional messages, keep digital proof that customers agreed to receive them. Operators check your list against consent and DND records, and drop numbers that do not qualify.

Skipping these steps means your messages will simply fail. For a broader introduction, read our guide to a bulk SMS service in India.

Writing SMS Messages That Convert

A standard SMS allows 160 characters, or 70 characters if you use Unicode for languages like Hindi, so every word counts.

Keep It Action-Oriented

Customers should never wonder why you texted them. Open with a hook, state the value and end with a clear call to action. Instead of "We have a new collection," try "Upgrade your wardrobe! See the new summer collection and use code SUN20 for 20% off."

Use Personalisation

Generic messages look like spam. With approved variable placeholders, you can add the customer's name, order number or a personal code. A message such as "Hi Rahul, your order #9876 is out for delivery" builds trust straight away.

Track and Improve

Add tracking parameters to links in your promotional messages so you can see how many people clicked and bought. Check that any link you use is allowed in your approved template, since operators can block unapproved links.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Sending promotional content through a transactional route.
  • Changing template text without getting the new version approved.
  • Messaging people who never gave consent.
  • Scheduling promotions outside the allowed window.
