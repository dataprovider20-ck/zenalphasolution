Contracts, IP and Data Access for Developers Working for Berlin Companies

Hiring a dedicated developer from outside Germany raises practical questions about contracts, ownership and access to data. It is better to settle them before the first line of code.

A written agreement should set out what is delivered, how the work is directed and what happens at the end. It should say that the work belongs to your company once paid for, and that confidential information stays confidential. An NDA covers the period before the contract starts. Your own lawyer should review the terms, since we cannot give you legal advice.

Data access deserves a separate rule. Developers do not need production customer data for most tasks. Give them test data or anonymised copies, and grant access to live systems only when required, for a limited time and with a record of who did what. This is good practice under European data protection law, and it reduces your risk if a laptop is lost.

Finally, decide how you will manage the relationship. Some companies prefer to give direction day to day, as with an employee. Others prefer to set outcomes and let a team lead manage the details. Both models work, and the right one depends on your team. We explain the options and adapt to how you prefer to work.

What Berlin teams ask us

Can the developer attend our Berlin daily?

Easily. A ten o'clock daily in Berlin happens in the early afternoon in New Delhi, well inside the developer's working hours.

Can we hire part-time?

Yes, for maintenance or smaller products, with fixed hours agreed in advance.

Do developers need German?

Usually not, since most Berlin tech teams work in English. Tell us if your product needs German content work.

Who owns what the developer builds?

Your startup does, provided the agreement says so, and ours is drafted that way. Read the clause with your lawyer before signing.
